Evaluate

A 68-year-old woman with hypothyroidism has a TSH of 7.2 mIU/L, and the nurse practitioner raises her levothyroxine from 75 to 88 mcg daily. When should her TSH be rechecked?

  1. In 6 to 8 weeks
  2. In 2 weeks
  3. In 12 months, at her annual wellness visit
  4. In 6 months

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. Levothyroxine has a half-life of about 7 days, so a new steady state takes about 5 to 6 weeks; the TSH is rechecked 6 to 8 weeks after any dose change.
  • B. Too soon. The new dose has not reached steady state at 2 weeks, so the TSH would still reflect the old dose and could prompt a needless second change.
  • C. Yearly TSH suits a long-stable patient. After a dose change it delays finding a dose that is still wrong; in an older adult, overtreatment that goes unnoticed raises the risk of atrial fibrillation and bone loss.
  • D. Six to 12 months is the interval once she is stable on a dose, not after a change. Waiting this long leaves an over- or under-replaced dose unchecked.

Levothyroxine has a long half-life (about 7 days), so serum levels and the pituitary TSH response take about 6 weeks to settle after a dose change. The ATA advises a TSH 6 to 8 weeks after starting or changing the dose, then titrating again if needed. Once the TSH is in range on a stable dose, monitoring moves to every 6 to 12 months. In older adults the target is often a little higher, and overtreatment should be avoided because it raises the risk of atrial fibrillation and osteoporosis.

Diagnose

A 54-year-old woman comes to the clinic 30 hours after a sudden, severe occipital headache that reached its peak within a minute while she was lifting a heavy box. It has eased to a moderate ache, and she has mild neck stiffness. She has no history of headaches, her neurologic examination is nonfocal, and BP is 148/90 mm Hg. The NP arranges emergency department transfer. Which diagnostic sequence is MOST appropriate?

  1. Lumbar puncture alone, because CT adds little after 24 hours
  2. Noncontrast head CT, then lumbar puncture if the CT is normal
  3. Noncontrast head CT alone, since a normal scan rules out hemorrhage
  4. Outpatient MRI of the brain with contrast within a week

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. CT still detects many bleeds after 24 hours and shows mass lesions or hydrocephalus that make lumbar puncture unsafe, so it comes first.
  • B. Correct. CT is the first test for suspected subarachnoid hemorrhage, but after 6 hours its sensitivity falls, so a normal scan is followed by lumbar puncture for red cells and xanthochromia.
  • C. A normal modern CT within 6 hours of onset effectively excludes subarachnoid hemorrhage, but she is 30 hours out, when a small bleed can be missed.
  • D. Improvement does not exclude a sentinel bleed from an aneurysm; a thunderclap headache needs same-day emergency evaluation, not scheduled imaging.

A thunderclap headache, one that reaches maximal intensity within a minute, is a red flag for subarachnoid hemorrhage, reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome, cervical artery dissection and other secondary causes, and needs emergency evaluation even when it improves. Noncontrast head CT is the first test. Its sensitivity is near 100% within 6 hours when read by an experienced radiologist but falls steadily after that, so a normal CT at 30 hours requires another test: lumbar puncture looking for red cells that do not clear and xanthochromia, or CT angiography. Exertion at onset and neck stiffness add to the concern here.

Plan

A 77-year-old woman has a painful band of grouped vesicles on a red base wrapping from her left mid-back to her left upper abdomen; the rash appeared 36 hours ago. There is no eye or ear involvement. She has chronic kidney disease, weighs 58 kg, and her estimated creatinine clearance is 38 mL/min. She has never had a zoster vaccine. Which prescription is MOST appropriate?

  1. Valacyclovir 1 g every 12 hours for 7 days
  2. Valacyclovir 1 g every 8 hours for 7 days
  3. Recombinant zoster vaccine today in place of an antiviral
  4. Valacyclovir 1 g once daily for 7 days

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. The label adjusts the zoster dose to 1 g every 12 hours for a creatinine clearance of 30 to 49 mL/min, and she is within the window in which antivirals work best.
  • B. This is the standard zoster dose for normal kidney function; at her clearance it accumulates and raises the risk of confusion, hallucinations and acute kidney injury.
  • C. The vaccine prevents future episodes but does not treat active shingles; it is given after the rash has resolved.
  • D. Once daily is the adjustment for a creatinine clearance of 10 to 29 mL/min; at 38 mL/min it underdoses her.

Antiviral therapy for herpes zoster shortens viral shedding and acute pain and is most effective when started within 48 to 72 hours of rash onset, especially in adults over 50. Valacyclovir is cleared by the kidneys, and the label gives the zoster dose as 1 g every 8 hours with normal function, every 12 hours at a creatinine clearance of 30 to 49, every 24 hours at 10 to 29, and 500 mg daily below 10 mL/min. Older adults with reduced clearance are prone to acyclovir neurotoxicity, so dosing and hydration matter. Once the rash heals she should receive the two-dose recombinant zoster vaccine, recommended for all adults 50 and older, including those with prior zoster.

Evaluate

A 47-year-old woman found a lump in her right breast 5 weeks ago. The NP feels a 2-cm hard, irregular, fixed mass in the upper outer quadrant that has not changed since her first visit, when she was midway through her menstrual cycle. Diagnostic mammography and targeted ultrasound of the area were both read as negative (BI-RADS 1). She has no family history of breast cancer. Which step is MOST appropriate NEXT?

  1. Reexamine the breast after her next menstrual period
  2. Repeat the diagnostic ultrasound in 6 months
  3. Refer to a breast surgeon for tissue diagnosis
  4. Reassure her and resume yearly screening mammography

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Reexamining after menses helps sort cyclic nodularity in a premenopausal woman, but this mass is discrete, hard, fixed and unchanged across her cycle.
  • B. Short-interval imaging is the plan for a probably benign finding (BI-RADS 3) or a low-suspicion exam, not for a hard, fixed, irregular mass.
  • C. Correct. A clinically suspicious mass needs tissue diagnosis even when mammography and ultrasound are negative, because imaging misses a small share of palpable cancers.
  • D. Negative imaging lowers the chance of cancer but does not exclude it; returning to routine screening leaves a clinically suspicious mass without a diagnosis.

Evaluation of a palpable breast mass rests on the clinical exam, imaging and, when needed, tissue: the so-called triple test. A hard, irregular, fixed mass is suspicious on exam alone, and when the clinical and imaging findings do not agree, the clinical finding governs; mammography and ultrasound together miss a small percentage of palpable cancers. Negative imaging is reassuring only when the exam is also low suspicion, in which case clinical follow-up in a few months is reasonable. The USPSTF screening schedule (biennial mammography from age 40 to 74) applies to women without symptoms, not to the workup of a mass.

The exams

NP Basics covers AANPCB FNP, ANCC FNP. Switch exams any time; mock exams follow each exam's length, time limit and topic proportions.

AANPCB

Family Nurse Practitioner (FNP)

500+ practice questions · 150-question mock exams, 3 h limit

The AANPCB Family Nurse Practitioner exam has 150 multiple-choice questions (135 scored and 15 unscored pretest questions) with a 3-hour limit. Practice-domain proportions follow the AANPCB FNP blueprint: assess, diagnose, plan and evaluate.

ANCC

Family Nurse Practitioner (FNP-BC)

600+ practice questions · 175-question mock exams, 3 h 30 min limit

The ANCC Family Nurse Practitioner (FNP-BC) exam has 175 questions (150 scored and 25 unscored pretest questions) with a 3.5-hour limit. Domain proportions follow the ANCC test content outline effective October 30, 2026, in which implementation (including legal, ethical and professional practice) is a third of the exam.
